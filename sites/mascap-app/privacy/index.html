1. Who we are

MASCAP Hospitals (34 Adeola Road, Sapele, Delta State) runs this system to care for its patients. Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (the Act), the hospital is the data controller: it decides what is kept and why, and it answers for it.

StellaXafe Technologies Limited, registered in Nigeria with the Corporate Affairs Commission, built the system and looks after it for the hospital. StellaXafe is a data processor: it works only on the hospital's instructions and never uses the information for itself.

This policy explains what the system keeps about patients and staff, why, who can see it, how long it is kept, and the rights every person has.

2. What we keep about patients

  • Who you are: name, patient number, date of birth, sex, phone, email, address, and your next of kin.
  • For antenatal care only: marital status, religion, level of education and occupation, and the same for your next of kin, with their age, as the antenatal card asks.
  • Your care: visits, vital signs, conditions, allergies, consultation notes, antenatal visits, nursing notes, medicines given, prescriptions, lab requests and results, appointments, admissions, and files added to your record (such as scans and old paper files).
  • Payments: what was paid, for what, and how (cash, POS or transfer).

Health information is sensitive personal data under the Act. It is kept only to give you care and is protected more strictly than anything else in the system.

3. What we keep about staff

  • Name, work email, role, room, and what each person is allowed to do.
  • The PIN is never stored. Only a scrambled fingerprint of it is kept, which cannot be turned back into the PIN.
  • Sign in and sign out times, and a log of what each person did (for example, who opened which record).
  • Messages between staff, and the devices that receive call alerts.
  • A computer or phone remembers the work email addresses that signed in on it, so people do not retype them. They stay on that device only, and anybody can remove one on the sign in screen.

We do not sell, rent or share information for advertising. The system has no adverts and no tracking, and it does not build profiles of anybody.

5. Who can see it

  • Only staff the hospital has invited can sign in, each with their own email code and their own PIN.
  • Each person sees only what their job needs. The medical record opens only for staff with medical record access, and every time a record is opened, it is written in the activity log. The laboratory sees lab requests, not the medical history.
  • These rules are enforced inside the database itself, not only on the screen.
  • StellaXafe staff may reach the system only to keep it working or to fix a fault, and only for that.

6. Where it is stored

  • The records are held by established cloud service providers that StellaXafe chose for their security. Each one works under a written agreement and may use the information only to provide its service.
  • The email service that sends sign in codes and staff invitations receives no medical information for them.
  • The same email service carries a patient's medical record or receipt only when a doctor, nurse or the front desk sends it, and only because the patient gave us their email address. It goes to that address and no other, and every send is written in the activity log. Once it is in the patient's inbox, keeping it private is up to the patient.
  • A copy of the database is made every 2 hours, locked with a passphrase, and stored separately, so the records survive a fault.
  • Once a month, when the hospital switches it on, a text copy of all the records is saved in the hospital's own online storage account. Pictures and files are not in it. Only the hospital can open that account, and switching it on or off is written in the activity log.
  • So that doctors and nurses can keep working when the network is down, the records of the patients seen in their own room in the last 7 days (at most 50) are kept, scrambled, on the devices of signed in clinical staff. Pictures and files are not kept. The copy is deleted when the person signs out, and after 24 hours. Keeping it, and every record opened from it, is written in the activity log.
  • Anything typed while the network is down waits on that device, and is sent as soon as the network is back.
  • The service that checks the app is answering receives no personal information.

Some of this storage may be outside Nigeria. Information leaves Nigeria only to places and providers with strong data protection rules, and only as the Act allows. The hospital keeps the list of providers and where they store information, and gives it to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission when asked.

7. How long we keep it

  • Medical and payment records are kept for as long as Nigerian law and medical practice rules require the hospital to keep them.
  • Messages between staff are deleted after one year.
  • Nightly copies of the database are deleted after 35 days.
  • When a staff member leaves, their account is switched off. What they recorded stays, with their name on it, because medical records must show who did what.

8. How we protect it

  • Everything travels encrypted (https), and the providers encrypt it where it is stored.
  • Sign in codes work for 10 minutes. Invitations run out after 72 hours. Five wrong PINs lock the account.
  • The screen locks when it is left, and each person signs out at the end of a shift.
  • The activity log records who opened and changed records.
  • Every copy is checked as it is made, to make sure it opens and holds every record.

9. Your rights

Under the Act, every patient and every member of staff can ask the hospital to:

  • tell them what is kept about them, and give them a copy;
  • correct anything that is wrong;
  • delete or stop using information, where the law allows (medical records the law requires the hospital to keep cannot be deleted early);
  • give their information to them, or to another hospital, in a usable form;
  • object to a use of their information, or withdraw a consent they gave.

To ask, speak to the front desk or email info@rhemamedical.app. The hospital answers within the time the Act allows. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC).

10. If something goes wrong

If personal information is lost, stolen or seen by somebody who should not see it, the hospital tells the Nigeria Data Protection Commission within 72 hours, as the Act requires, and tells the people affected when there is a high risk to them. StellaXafe tells the hospital straight away when it finds such a problem.

11. Children

A child's record is kept the same way, and their rights are used by a parent or guardian.
